How Much Does It Cost to Charge an Electric Scooter Monthly in Morocco?

📅 7 November 2026 • ⏱️ 5 minute read • ✍️ VoltRide team • Economics

Charging an electric scooter is very cheap compared to fuel, but the exact figure depends on your electricity price per kilowatt‑hour and your battery capacity. Rather than impose a price that might be outdated, we give you the equation and an example with adjustable numbers.

1. The Equation

Charge cost = Energy drawn from the wall (kWh) × electricity price per kilowatt‑hour

Energy drawn = battery energy ÷ charging efficiency. If charging efficiency is 85–90%, energy drawn from the wall is approximately 11–18% higher than the battery's rated energy, because wall energy = battery energy ÷ charging efficiency.

Battery energy in kilowatt‑hours = (volts × amp-hours) ÷ 1000.

2. Example: 36-volt × 12.8 amp-hour Battery

  • Battery energy = 36 × 12.8 ÷ 1000 ≈ 0.46 kWh.
  • Allowing for approximately 15% additional energy drawn from the wall ≈ 0.53 kWh is pulled from the wall.
  • If the electricity price is 1.20 MAD/kWh: Cost ≈ 0.63 MAD per full charge.

3. Another Example: Larger Battery Around 1.5 kWh

Energy drawn ≈ 1.5 × 1.15 ≈ 1.7 kWh.

4. Where to Find Your Rate?

  • Your electricity bill is calculated using progressive tiers, so the higher your monthly consumption, the higher the price per kilowatt‑hour.
  • Prices vary slightly by distributor and subscription type, and include fees and tax.
  • Add scooter charging to your monthly consumption; it may shift you to a higher tier. The difference is usually small because scooter consumption is low compared to household use.
  • Enter your price in Settings ← Electricity Tariff in VoltRide to calculate cost automatically. For battery care tips see 5 Common Mistakes That Shorten Battery Life.

5. Comparing to Gasoline

Compare using one calculation: cost per 100 km with electricity = (watt-hours per km × 100 ÷ 1000 ÷ charging efficiency) × electricity price per kWh. Cost per 100 km with gasoline = scooter fuel consumption (L/100 km) × fuel price per liter today. Enter current figures yourself and you’ll see the difference—electricity is usually far cheaper.

Note

These are approximate calculations. Cost does not include scooter purchase, battery degradation, or maintenance, which are part of the real cost of travel.
