Best 5 Scooter Accessories

📅 October 15, 2026 • Updated November 12 • ⏱️ 4 min read • ✍️ VoltRide Team • Guide

A good accessory doesn't change your scooter, but it protects you and protects your investment. This is a concise buyer's guide: for each accessory , check what to verify before paying, not just a list of brand names. Note: Check local regulations for specific safety requirements and legal standards.

1. Helmet: The Priority First Choice

The helmet is the most important piece for your safety. Check local regulations for helmet requirements.

  • Has a recognized safety standard mark (like EN 1078 for European bicycle helmets).
  • Fits your head without movement, and the chin strap is tight enough.
  • Has reasonable ventilation and a removable/cleanable liner.
  • Openings don't block your hearing.
  • New and undamaged. Replace a helmet that has taken a significant hit, even if it appears fine.

2. Strong Lock

  • Prefer a D-lock (U-lock) or hardened chain link. Thin cables are easy to cut.
  • Lock the wheel to a fixed object, not just the wheel itself.
  • The size should fit the places you usually stop, and the weight shouldn't be burdensome.
  • A second lock in high-risk areas increases the difficulty for thieves, but doesn't replace a good primary lock.

3. Tires

  • The size (diameter and width) must match what the manufacturer specifies for your model. Check the manual.
  • Pneumatic tires are more comfortable but can puncture; solid tires can't be punctured but are harder on rough roads.
  • Worn or punctured tires should be replaced—they affect traction and range.
  • Installation: seek a specialist if you're not experienced, as some wheels use special tools.

4. Charger

  • The voltage and current should exactly match the battery specifications printed on the label. Don't rely on just fitting into the connector.
  • Better to use the original charger or one approved by the manufacturer, and avoid unknown sources.
  • Cable should be intact with no cracks, and the charge indicator should be clear.
  • A second charger is useful when you charge in multiple locations, provided it matches completely.

5. Lighting

  • Clear white front light for the road, red rear light for visibility to others.
  • Sideways visibility is also important: reflectors on the wheel or a reflective jacket.
  • Chargeable or battery-powered lighting is fine, with secure mounting that withstands vibration.
  • Make sure the installation doesn't interfere with brakes or the throttle.
