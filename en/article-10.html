Electric Scooter Accessories Buying Guide: What to Check Before Purchase

📅 October 26, 2026 • ⏱️ 7 min read • ✍️ VoltRide Team • Beginner Guide

The right accessories help protect you, your scooter and your investment. This guide focuses on what to check before you pay, rather than promoting unverified products or brands. We may add clearly labelled affiliate links in the future. For legal context, see our article on Moroccan regulations.

⛑️ 1. Helmet

A suitable helmet is an important part of personal protection. Check the official legal text for requirements that apply to your vehicle category; do not rely on press summaries alone.

  • Choose a helmet that conforms to a recognized safety standard appropriate to its intended use. EN 1078 is an example for bicycle, skateboard and roller-skate helmets; CE marking alone is not a helmet safety standard.
  • Choose the correct size: the helmet should sit securely without moving, and its chin strap should fasten properly.
  • Look for reasonable ventilation, a cleanable lining and a design that does not obstruct your hearing.
  • Use a new, undamaged helmet. Replace it after a strong impact, even if no damage is visible.

🔒 2. Lock

  • Prefer a U-lock or reinforced chain. Thin cables can be cut easily.
  • Secure the scooter's frame to a fixed, solid object; locking only a removable wheel may not be enough.
  • Choose a size that fits the places where you usually park, without making the lock impractical to carry.
  • A second lock can make theft more difficult in higher-risk areas, but cannot eliminate the risk.

🛞 3. Tyres

  • Match the tyre diameter and width to the manufacturer's specification for your exact scooter model.
  • Pneumatic tyres can provide more comfort but may puncture; solid tyres resist punctures but can feel harsher on uneven roads.
  • Replace tyres that are worn, cracked or repeatedly losing pressure. Tyre condition affects grip and may affect range.
  • If you are not experienced with the procedure, have a qualified technician fit the tyre; some wheel assemblies require specialist tools.

🔌 4. Charger

  • Verify the charger's output voltage, current, connector and polarity against the scooter manufacturer's requirements and the original charger label.
  • Prefer the original charger or a replacement approved for your exact model. Avoid unknown or unverified chargers.
  • Check that the cable and casing are undamaged and that the charging indicator works as described by the manufacturer.
  • A second charger may be useful at another location, but only if it matches the scooter and battery specifications exactly.

💡 5. Lighting

  • Use a white front light to illuminate the road and a red rear light so other road users can see you.
  • Side visibility matters too: consider suitable reflectors on the scooter or reflective clothing.
  • Choose rechargeable lights or a manufacturer-supported scooter-powered option, with a secure mount that withstands vibration.
  • Make sure installation does not obstruct the brakes, controls or steering.
