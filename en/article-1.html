10 Tips to Extend Your Electric Scooter's Range

📅 October 12, 2026 • Updated October 20 • ⏱️ 5 min read • ✍️ VoltRide Team • Tips

Many riders notice their real-world range is less than the number printed on the box. The advertised number is typically measured with a light rider, on flat roads, at a steady speed. The real world is different. Here are ten habits that can actually improve your range, and some of them may show immediate results.

1. Adjust Your Tire Pressure

A flat tire increases rolling resistance and makes the motor work harder. Check pressure every two weeks or so and stick to the value printed on the tire or in the company's manual, as it differs between models.

2. Lighten Your Load

Every additional kilogram (passenger or bag) increases consumption, especially on hills. Don't carry what you don't need, and remember that VoltRide's range estimate is affected by your weight.

3. Maintain a Steady, Moderate Speed

Air resistance increases with speed, so consumption rises sharply. Repeated acceleration and hard braking also wastes energy. The maximum legal speed in Morocco is 25 km/h (15.5 mph) anyway, so staying there constantly isn't useful if you want longer range.

4. Use Eco Mode

Most scooters offer an eco/slow mode (Eco) that limits acceleration and power. Use it on long rides and keep sport mode for when you actually need it.

5. Choose a Lower-Incline Path

Hills are the biggest battery drain. If there are two routes close in distance, take the lower one. And on a powerful scooter with a weaker motor, you might find walking down and pushing the scooter safer than burning out the motor.

6. Maintain Brakes and Wheels

Braking continuously by the disc or dirty pads steals energy without you noticing. Inspect brakes and wheels regularly, and visit a technician if you hear any unusual scraping sounds.

7. Charge with Battery-Care Habits

Many companies recommend avoiding leaving the battery fully charged or empty for long periods, and using the original charger. Read your manufacturer's recommendation for your specific model (see also our article 5 Common Mistakes That Shorten Battery Life).

8. Beware of Extreme Heat and Cold

High temperatures can damage your battery long-term, and cold temporarily reduces available range. In summer, don't park the scooter in direct sun, and in winter store it in a moderate environment. That's why VoltRide includes a weather adjustment factor.

9. Replace Worn Tires

Worn or punctured tires are less safe and efficient. Replace them when you see the wear marks the manual recommends.

10. Learn Your Real Range and Don't Rely on Box Numbers

The best method is measuring: log your trip distance and the battery percentage it used. VoltRide's "reality factor" learns from your trips and makes your pre-ride range estimates more accurate.

Alert

Results from these tips vary by model and route, and aren't a fixed percentage. Always use a safety margin to avoid needing to carry your scooter a long distance.
