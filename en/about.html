About VoltRide

VoltRide is a free tool for electric scooter riders, designed first for Morocco. It answers the questions riders ask every day: how far will my scooter go? Will I get back safely? How much does a charge cost?

A note from the founder

I'm passionate about electric micromobility. I noticed that scooter riders in Morocco and Europe lack accurate information about real-world range and charging cost, which leads to constant worry (range anxiety).

So I decided to build VoltRide: a platform that combines software engineering with real-world data. I don't make marketing promises; I offer a calculation tool built on physics and mathematics to help you plan your ride safely.

My goal is to make VoltRide the first reference for anyone who owns an electric scooter: your trusted guide before you buy, and before you ride.

— The founder of VoltRide

What VoltRide offers

  • Range calculator: estimates range, energy use, charging time and cost from your weight, speed, road type and riding style.
  • 🧭 Return Check: before you leave, enter the distance, battery level and weather, and the app tells you whether the trip is safe, tight, or not recommended.
  • Reality factor: after your real rides you log distance and battery used, and the app corrects its estimates for your own scooter.
  • My scooter and charge history: save your scooter's specs and track charging cost over time.
  • Catalog and comparison: approximate reference specs and prices for popular models.
  • Safety center and first-step troubleshooting, in three languages: Arabic, French and English.
  • Free, no account, and your data stays on your device.

How do we calculate? (method)

The model is simplified and approximate. It works like this:

  1. Energy: battery energy = volts × amp-hours. We treat 90% of it as usable.
  2. Consumption: we start from a base value of about 12.5 Wh per kilometre, then adjust for rider weight (compared with 75 kg), speed above 20 km/h, road type (flat, mixed, hilly), riding style (eco, balanced, sport) and motor power.
  3. Range = usable energy ÷ consumption. The "reserve" figure is 90% of the range.
  4. Charging time ≈ battery capacity (Ah) ÷ charger current (A), plus 15% for the absorption phase and losses.
  5. Cost = battery energy in kWh × the electricity tariff you enter in Settings.
  6. Return Check multiplies the range by the "reality factor" learned from your rides and by a weather factor (cold reduces range), then compares what you have with what you need.

Limits of the tool

These are estimates, not laboratory measurements. The result is affected by wind, temperature, tyre pressure, battery condition, braking and repeated acceleration. Always leave a safety margin, and don't rely on the tool alone for a trip you couldn't finish on foot.
